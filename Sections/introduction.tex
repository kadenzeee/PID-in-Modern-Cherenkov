Modern high energy physics experiments rely on the performance of detector systems to accurately study collision events. Cherenkov 
imaging detectors will play a central role in the upcoming generation of
experiments in quantum chromodynamics, gluon research, and high-energy neutrinos. 
Improving the capabilities of these imaging detectors is the work
of thousands of physicists and engineers around the world.

This review aims to provide an introduction to the field of detectors making use of Cherenkov radiation for particle identification (PID). We will cover the basic principles of Cherenkov radiation, some design choices of Cherenkov imaging detectors, and briefly the latest advancements in technology. Cherenkov imaging detectors can be broadly divided into particle accelerator-based systems, such as RICH (Ring Imagaing Cherenkov) and DIRC (Detection of Internally Reflected Cherenkov) detectors, and large-volume systems, such as large-volume water or ice Cherenkov detectors employed in neutrino and astroparticle physics.

We will begin with an overview of Cherenkov radiation
in Sect \nolinebreak\ref{sec:cherenkov-radiation}, and then introduce photomultipliers in Sect \nolinebreak\ref{sec:photomultipliers}. Following this, we will
delve into accelerator-based Cherenkov imaging detectors in Sect \nolinebreak\ref{sec:cherenkov-imaging-detectors}, 
where over the chapters we will discuss their designs and differences. Then, an astrophysics application to a topological Cherenkov tracking detector is introduced in Sect \ref{sec:topological-detectors}. Finally, 
we will discuss some of the main points of open research in Sect \nolinebreak\ref{sec:current-challenges}.
