% Content for the "SiPMs" subsection

Silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) have been gaining traction recently due to 
developments in materials, leading to a great ease of manufacturing. They are small compared to an MCP-PMT, due to their use of single photon avalanche diodes (SPADs), each containing
an avalanche photodiode and a quenching resistor. 
\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{Images/sipm.png}
\caption{\textbf{Cross section of SiPM.} SiPMs are externally biased such that 
each avalanche photodiode is at breakdown voltage, allowing for electron
avalanches. The gain from an SiPM roughly matches that of a MCP-PMT at \(10^5\)–\(10^6\) amplification\nolinebreak\cite{hamamatsu_2016}.}\label{fig:traditional-sipm}
\end{figure}

SiPMs are overall cheaper per unit area than MCP-PMTs including all cryogenic and electrical supporting systems, and have a higher QE, however suffer from a high dark count rate and do not necessarily have similar hit rate capabilities, as the hit rate is mainly dependent on the density of SPADs. This
has led to a surge of SiPMs being used in fields like positron emission photography (PET)
where precise single-photon timings are not necessary, and where ruggedness and sturdiness
is required. SiPMs also operate at a far lower voltage than MCP-PMTs and are not affected
by magnetic fields, adding to their dependability in medical settings.

The high dark count rate degrades PID in extremely precise systems, as noise causes statistical event reconstruction methods to struggle. This is mitigated with cryogenic cooling, however still remain around the \(\text{MHz}/\text{cm}^2\) level, compared to the MCP-PMTs at only a few \(\text{Hz}/\text{cm}^2\).

Also, the dead time of SiPMs poses thought-provoking problems for systems which rely on precise single-photon arrival time for strong PID performance. The typical dead time of one SPAD is between 10–50ns \nolinebreak\cite{Bruschini2019},
so even though the microcells are much smaller than an MCP-PMT pixel, in the particularly photon-dense areas of the detector, it is possible to entirely lose some photons due to the dead time of the SiPM.

SiPMs provide an interesting alternative to MCP-PMTs, where their low cost and robust 
characteristics make them an extremely lucrative option for many applications in photon imaging. Their 
performance in high-energy physics applications is certainly going to improve in the future, and 
they are already a serious consideration for future LHCb RICH upgrades.
